Selling The Oreo Lady on GoHighLevel

How Tiffany goes from "DM me and hope" to a business that books, bills, and blasts texts by itself.

Prepared for Michael · Prospect: Tiffany Ponds, The Oreo Lady (Lakeland / 863)

The one-liner

What you're actually selling

You're not selling her software. You're selling her a line of customers that forms before she even parks — and a business that runs while her hands are busy frying.

Every feature maps back to one of three promises. If a sentence out of your mouth doesn't ladder up to one of these, cut it:

  • "They find you." — schedule page, text alerts, "she's in your area" blasts.
  • "They pay you without you stopping." — invoices, tap-to-pay, deposits for event bookings.
  • "They book you while you sleep." — a calendar link instead of 40 back-and-forth DMs.

The emotional core

Her product already sells itself — the flyer says "come hungry" and people do. The bottleneck is that she is the marketing department, booking system, and cashier, and all three shut down the second the fryer gets busy. GHL is her hiring three employees for less than one day of sales a month.

Worksheet — fill this in while you talk

Discovery questions (get her telling the story)

Don't pitch yet. Ask, write, nod. Her answers become your pitch.

1. How many spots do you set up at in a normal week? Which days are biggest?

2. How do people find out where you are right now? How many ask and miss you?

3. When someone wants to book you for a party or a business pop-up, what happens, step by step?

4. Ever had someone book and ghost? Did you have a deposit?

5. How do people pay you today — cash, Cash App, card? Ever lose a sale because of payment?

6. If 200 people near Lakeland could get a text the morning you set up — what would Saturday look like?

7. What's an average order ($10 boxes — how many per customer)? How many customers on a good day vs. slow day?

8. How much time per week goes to DMs, scheduling posts, and chasing bookings?

Act III — the pitch, shot for shot

The pitch script (play it like a movie)

1

Cold open — show, don't tell (2 min)

Open the new website on your phone and hand it to her. Say nothing. Let her scroll her own brand looking like a movie.

You say"This is already yours. Now let me show you the machine that goes behind it."
2

The villain — her Tuesday (3 min)

Replay her own discovery answers back to her: the DMs, the missed calls, the ghosted bookings. She should be nodding "yes, that's exactly it."

You say"Every one of those is you doing a job a robot should be doing while you're trying to fry."
3

The demo — one text, one line (5 min)

Live demo with HER phone: put her number in the form, then send the broadcast — "🍪 Oreo Lady is posted up at [spot] til 4!" Her phone buzzes in her hand. That buzz is the sale.

Then book a fake party on the calendar and show the deposit invoice hit. Two moments, that's the whole demo.

4

The math — her numbers, not yours (3 min)

Do the napkin-math worksheet above together, in front of her, with her numbers.

5

The close — small yes (2 min)

You say"Let's not boil the ocean. Month one: your text list, your booking link, your missed-call catcher. I set it all up — you just keep frying. If it doesn't put money in your pocket in 30 days, we shut it off."

Then be quiet. First one to talk loses.

Your side of the table

How to package & price it (options for you)

Option A — Done-for-you retainer (recommended)

$250–$500 setup + $150–$300/mo

  • You run a GHL agency account; she's a sub-account
  • You own the build, she pays for outcomes
  • Include X broadcasts/mo + booking upkeep

Option B — SaaS mode

$97–$197/mo self-serve

  • She gets her own login, you white-label it
  • Lower touch, but she's "not techy" — riskier churn
  • Better later, once she's hooked on the results
Cost reality check (verify current pricing before you quote): GHL agency plans have historically run ~$97/mo (Starter) and ~$297/mo (Unlimited, lets you spin up unlimited sub-accounts — the one you want if Tiffany is client #1 of many). SMS/voice usage through LC Phone bills per-segment on top (fractions of a cent per text; a 200-person blast costs roughly a couple of dollars). A2P 10DLC registration is required for business texting in the US — build the ~1–2 week approval into your timeline and don't promise texts on day one.

The 30-day rollout you promise her

  • Week 1 — Sub-account, phone number, A2P registration filed, website connected, QR "join the list" cards printed
  • Week 2 — Booking calendar + deposit invoicing live; missed-call text-back on
  • Week 3 — First text blast to the early list; schedule workflow running
  • Week 4 — Review the numbers together: list size, bookings, revenue. Renew on results.
